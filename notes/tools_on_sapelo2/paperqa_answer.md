# How do orphan or lineage-specific genes arise, and how much of the apparent orphan count is due to homologs that sequence search failed to detect rather than true de novo origin? What methods do these papers use to tell real young genes from annotation artifacts or undetected homologs?

Question: How do orphan or lineage-specific genes arise, and how much of the apparent orphan count is due to homologs that sequence search failed to detect rather than true de novo origin? What methods do these papers use to tell real young genes from annotation artifacts or undetected homologs?

Orphan and lineage-specific genes arise primarily through two mechanisms: divergence of ancestral genes beyond sequence recognition, and de novo emergence from non-genic sequences (PMC7028367 chunk 2). Additional processes include gene duplication followed by rapid divergence, retroposition, horizontal gene transfer, and gene loss (PMC4383367 chunk 1, PMC6542195 chunk 5, PMC3590893 chunk 12). Synteny-based analyses indicate that sequence divergence accounts for at most one-third of apparent orphan genes, with the majority attributed to de novo origin (PMC7028367 chunk 13, PMC7028367 chunk 2). Phylostratigraphy simulations similarly report that undetected homologs represent 11–14% of genes in yeast and Drosophila (PMC6542195 chunk 3).

To distinguish genuine young genes from annotation artifacts or undetected homologs, several methods are employed. Synteny-based pipelines identify candidate homologs by conserved genomic position irrespective of sequence similarity, bypassing BLAST limitations (PMC7028367 chunk 2, PMC7028367 chunk 13). These approaches confirm that the syntenic region lacks coding potential in outgroup species and can pinpoint the mutations that created coding capacity (PMC6542195 chunk 3). Complementary strategies include careful selection of BLAST E-value thresholds to balance false positives and negatives (PMC7028367 chunk 13), exclusion of genes with paralogs or conserved domains, and requirement of syntenic and expression evidence (PMC6542195 chunk 5). Validation against annotation artifacts involves replicating gene model searches across genomes (PMC3590893 chunk 12). Additionally, splitting genes by evolutionary rate and using gene loss metrics help ensure that rapid evolution does not artifactually inflate young gene counts (PMC2997544 chunk 11).
